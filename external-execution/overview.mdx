This page explains what the external execution lane is, who it is for, how to
get access, and what you have to have in place before your first order. When
you're ready to write code, go to [Signing & Order Lifecycle](/external-execution/signing).

The **external execution lane** lets an approved market maker or institutional desk trade through Kairos's execution infrastructure while holding its own **keys, gas, and funds** — you sign orders locally with your own externally-owned account ([EOA](/learn/glossary)); Kairos builds the exact payload, verifies your signature, and forwards it to the venue.

This is the low-latency, self-custody counterpart to the default Kairos path, where orders are signed for you by a managed key. **It exists for one reason: latency.** A managed signature makes a cross-region round-trip to a cloud HSM (~50–100 ms per order). On this lane you sign locally — sub-millisecond and free — so the only added latency is the network hop to your regional execution node, minimized with [regional colocation](/external-execution/regional-execution) and a persistent WebSocket signing channel. **Kairos never custodies your key and never moves funds you did not sign for.**

## What you bring vs. what Kairos does

| You bring | Kairos does |
|---|---|
| An EOA (any secp256k1 key — hardware wallet, HSM, hot key) | Builds the exact EIP-712 order payload for you to sign |
| Funds in that wallet (e.g. pUSD on Polygon for Polymarket) | Verifies your signature (`ecrecover`) against your registered wallet |
| Gas for on-chain operations (approvals, redemptions) | Submits to the venue, persists the order, tracks fills, positions, and PnL |
| A signing endpoint or an open WebSocket connection | Runs the regional matching / execution plumbing |

Every order Kairos submits on your behalf carries a signature that recovers to an address **registered to your account**. A signature over one order cannot be replayed onto another — the signed digest pins price, size, side, token, maker/signer, and venue (the verifying contract, which differs between standard and neg-risk markets). Expiration and `post_only` sit on the outer payload rather than inside Polymarket's signed `Order` struct; replay is instead foreclosed by the per-order `salt` and `timestamp`, and by single-use intents.

## The account model

You trade as a **pure EOA** (`signatureType = 0` on Polymarket): the wallet that signs is the wallet that makes the order and holds the funds. There is no Safe, proxy, or managed sub-org — the simplest, fully non-custodial shape. It signs orders (EIP-712), holds the settlement asset (e.g. pUSD on Polygon), and pays its own gas for any on-chain operation; owner, signer, and maker are always the same address.

## Access

This lane is **allow-listed per account**. Until Kairos approves your account, every external-execution endpoint returns:

**403 Forbidden:**
```json
{ "error": "external-signing execution is not enabled for this account" }
```

To request access, contact your Kairos account manager. Approval is a deliberate manual step so the allow-list stays small and intentional.

> **The gate is checked server-side on every request and fails closed.** If the check itself errors you get `403 external-signing authorization check failed` — never a `500`, never a pass-through. Both steps of a two-round-trip flow are gated independently, so an in-flight `payload_id` does not bypass the check at `/submit`.

> **Gotcha: revocation is prompt, not atomic.** The flag is served from a short-lived cache, so expect a change to take effect within a couple of seconds — longer if the central database is degraded. Do not build anything that assumes an instant cutoff.

## Getting started

1. **Get allow-listed.** Contact your Kairos account manager. Until approved, every call is `403`.
2. **Register your signing EOA(s).** Kairos only accepts signatures that recover to an address registered to your account. Send the address(es) during onboarding — you may register several (e.g. a primary and a backup HSM key); any of them can sign.
3. **Fund your wallet.** Deposit the venue's settlement asset into your EOA on the venue's chain (for Polymarket: **pUSD** on Polygon). You control the deposit entirely; there is no custody step.
4. **Set on-chain approvals.** Before the venue can move your collateral on a fill, your EOA needs one-time approvals (for Polymarket: `pUSD.approve` and `ConditionalTokens.setApprovalForAll` against the CTF Exchange, plus the NegRisk variants). Self-serve: call `POST /v2/onchain/intent` with `op: "approvals"`, sign each returned `signing_digest_hex` with your EOA, and post the signatures to `POST /v2/onchain/submit`. You supply the `nonce` and `gas_price_wei` and you pay the gas. Your account manager can also walk through this during onboarding.
5. **Keep a small gas balance.** Order placement and cancellation on Polymarket are off-chain and gasless; gas is only needed for the on-chain operations above (a few cents of POL for approvals, more only if you later redeem resolved positions).
6. **Get your credentials & node assignment.** Obtain your Kairos JWT (or a scoped API key issued at onboarding) and confirm which [regional execution node](/external-execution/regional-execution) you connect to for lowest latency.
7. **Place your first order.** Follow the [Signing & Order Lifecycle](/external-execution/signing) walkthrough.

### Onboarding checklist

- Account approved for external execution (via your account manager)
- Signing EOA(s) registered to your account
- Wallet funded with the venue settlement asset (e.g. pUSD on Polygon)
- On-chain approvals set (self-serve via `/v2/onchain/*`, or with your account manager)
- Small native-gas balance (POL) on hand for approvals and any future redemptions
- Kairos JWT / API key in hand
- Regional node / colocation confirmed with Kairos

## Security model in one paragraph

You authenticate to your regional node with a Kairos JWT (or scoped API key). Every order Kairos submits is reconstructed **server-side from stored inputs**, and its signature is `ecrecover`'d to an address that **must** be registered to your account. Intents are single-use, short-TTL, and bound to the account that created them. A leaked intent id is useless without the matching signature, and a signature over one payload cannot be replayed onto another. You never supply a contract address or calldata — the only thing Kairos submits is bytes you signed.
